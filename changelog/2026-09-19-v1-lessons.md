title: What version 1 taught us, and why the number is going away
promote: true
---

Version 1 worked: a menu-bar meter that shows your speaking speed live, accurate to within about 5% on test recordings. After a day with it, though, it was clear it would become wallpaper. A live number is fun to watch for a day, then you stop seeing it, and while you do watch it you lean on it instead of learning to feel your own pace. It also jumps around, because real speech varies from sentence to sentence, and no averaging window is both instant and steady.

The recordings taught us something else: when I "sped up" I mostly stopped pausing, rather than saying words faster. That fits the speech-therapy advice that the fix is to pause, not to talk slower. So version 2 hides the number behind a quiet dot, nudges only when you've gone about 15 seconds without a pause or have been fast for a sustained stretch, and asks "how did that feel?" after each conversation. Full write-up: [what we learned from version 1](https://github.com/rufuspollock/speaking-speed/blob/main/docs/v1-lessons.md); plan: [version 2 plan](https://github.com/rufuspollock/speaking-speed/blob/main/docs/plans/2026-09-19-v2-behavioural-feedback-plan.md).
